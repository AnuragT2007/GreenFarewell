About the Peepal Tree

The Peepal Tree (*Ficus religiosa*), also known as the sacred fig or Bodhi tree, is revered for its cultural, spiritual, and ecological significance. Native to South Asia, it is a symbol of longevity and wisdom.

Planted By: Ms. Vaidehi ma'am, Mr. Satish sir and Ms. Palve ma'am

Event Name: GreenFarewell 2025

Scientific Name

Species: *Ficus religiosa*

Family: Moraceae

Physical Characteristics

  • Size: Can grow up to 30 meters (98 feet) tall.
  • Leaves: Heart-shaped with an elongated tip.
  • Fruits: Small, round, and purple when ripe, providing food for birds and animals.

Ecological Importance

  • Acts as a natural air purifier by releasing oxygen.
  • Provides habitat and food for numerous birds and small animals.
  • Thrives in various climatic conditions and prevents soil erosion.

Health Benefits

  • Medicinal Uses: Treats skin disorders, respiratory issues, and digestive problems in Ayurveda.
  • Stress Relief: Spending time under the tree promotes mental clarity.
  • Oxygen Production: Produces oxygen even at night.

Cultural and Religious Significance

  • Hinduism: Associated with Lord Vishnu and Shiva, worshipped during rituals.
  • Buddhism: Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree, a type of Peepal tree.
  • Jainism: Revered in Jain scriptures and practices.
  • Symbolism: Represents longevity, wisdom, and life's interconnectedness.

Uses

  • Environmental: Prevents soil erosion and provides shade.
  • Medicinal: Bark powder and leaf decoctions treat wounds and diabetes.
  • Landscaping: Planted in roadsides and courtyards for shade.

Growing Conditions

  • Climate: Thrives in tropical and subtropical climates.
  • Soil: Adapts well to various soil types, including sandy and loamy soil.
  • Watering: Requires regular watering during early stages but becomes drought-tolerant when mature.
  • Sunlight: Needs full sunlight for optimal growth.

Interesting Facts

  • The Peepal tree is often considered immortal due to its long lifespan.
  • It is one of the first trees mentioned in Indian scriptures, like the Vedas.
  • Leaves are used in traditional art and crafts.
  • Its calming energy makes it a popular spot for meditation and reflection.
