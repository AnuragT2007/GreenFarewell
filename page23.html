Taxonomy & Classification

  • Scientific Name: Plumeria pudica Jacq.
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Order: Gentianales
  • Clade: Tracheophytes, Angiosperms, Eudicots, Asterids

Botanical Description

  • Growth Habit: Plumeria pudica is generally described as a deciduous shrub. It typically reaches heights of about 3 to 4 meters (roughly 10–13 feet), though various cultivars and growing conditions might result in a more compact form or, in some cases, a larger ornamental tree. The trunk tends to be thin, with multiple branches emerging at the base to form a dense and slightly flared crown
  • Leaves: One of the plant’s most distinctive features is its leaves. They are thick, shiny dark green, and uniquely spoon- or fiddle-shaped. Their unusual form—often compared to a cobra's hood—adds an exotic flair to the plant’s overall appearance. Keep in mind that, like many members of the Apocynaceae family, these leaves are toxic if ingested, making them a topic of interest both botanically and in terms of careful handling
  • Flowers: The blooms of Plumeria pudica are typically white with a yellow center. They tend to appear profusely and are often described as a “bridal bouquet” or “white frangipani” in common parlance. While the scent of the flowers is generally mild or even almost unscented, some cultivars show slight variation in fragrance. Moreover, selective breeding has yielded varieties with differences—notably a variegated-leaf type called “Golden Arrow” or “Gilded Spoon” and a pink-hued hybrid known as “Sri Supakorn” or “Pink pudica”

Common Names & Cultivars

Plumeria pudica is known by several common names, reflecting its popularity and varied appearances:

  • Wild Plumeria
  • Bridal Bouquet
  • White Frangipani
  • Lei Flower
  • Fiddle Leaf Plumeria
  • In local contexts, names like Thai champa or Naag (cobra) champa appear in South Asian languages.

Its diverse cultivars arise from both natural variations and deliberate hybridization. These variations offer gardeners choices in terms of flower color, leaf variegation, and overall growth form.

Native Range & Distribution

Originally, Plumeria pudica is native to parts of northern South America—with documented origins in Panama, Colombia, and Venezuela. Over time, thanks to its striking looks and adaptability, it has been widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions around the globe, including several parts of Asia where it is treasured as an ornamental plant.

Cultivation & Care

Successful growth and care for Plumeria pudica depend on understanding its native habitat and adapting those conditions as much as possible:

  • Light & Climate: This plant thrives in full sun to partial shade. It is best suited to warm climates—USDA hardiness zones 10–11—where it can bloom almost year-round. In cooler regions, it is often grown in containers that can be moved indoors during winter months.
  • Soil Requirements: Plumeria pudica does best in well-drained, loamy or sandy soil. A slightly acidic pH (around 5.5–6.0) is ideal, which helps prevent most root diseases and promotes healthy growth
  • Watering & Feeding: As a tropical species, it appreciates regular watering—usually about 1 inch per week—especially during the active growing season. However, its shallow root system means it is also susceptible to root disturbance, so good drainage is crucial. For plants grown in containers, a monthly feeding regime during the growing months can be beneficial.
  • Propagation: The most reliable method of propagation is via cuttings taken from healthy branches. While the plant can be grown from seeds, using cuttings better preserves the characteristics of the desired cultivar and typically results in a faster establishment

Additional Information & Uses

  • Biochemical Studies: Research has focused on the latex proteins extracted from Plumeria pudica. Studies using mice have shown promise in reducing ligature-induced periodontitis as well as acting as antioxidants. Such studies underscore the potential medicinal applications of its biochemical compounds, which is an emerging area of interest
  • Ornamental & Cultural Significance: Beyond its use in home gardens and landscaping, Plumeria pudica is deeply embedded in cultural practices. Frangipani flowers are frequently used in ceremonies, decorations, and religious events in tropical countries. Their symbolism—often associated with love, honor, and beauty—adds another layer to their appeal.
