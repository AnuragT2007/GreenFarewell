About Coral Jasmine

Also known as Parijat or Harsingar, the Coral Jasmine flower (*Nyctanthes arbor-tristis*) is a highly fragrant and beautiful flower with significant cultural and medicinal value. Native to South and Southeast Asia, it is cherished for its unique blooms and spiritual importance.

Scientific Classification

Botanical Name: *Nyctanthes arbor-tristis*

Family: Oleaceae

Common Names: Coral Jasmine, Parijat, Harsingar, Night Jasmine

Physical Characteristics

  • Plant Type: Small tree or shrub, up to 10 meters tall.
  • Leaves: Opposite, ovate, and rough-textured.
  • Flowers: White flowers with an orange-red center, fragrant, and bloom at night.
  • Fruit: Flat, brown, heart-shaped capsules containing seeds.

Cultural and Spiritual Significance

  • Hindu Mythology: Sacred and associated with legends; often used in temple rituals.
  • Festivals: Widely used in offerings during festivals like Durga Puja in Bengal.
  • Symbolism: Represents love, devotion, and spiritual awakening.

Medicinal Uses

  • Leaves: Used to treat arthritis, sciatica, and fever.
  • Flowers: Remedies for skin ailments and inflammation.
  • Seeds: Used as a hair tonic and for treating baldness.
  • Traditional Remedies: Decoctions help with bronchitis, cough, and intestinal worms.

Growing Conditions

  • Climate: Thrives in tropical and subtropical climates.
  • Soil: Prefers well-drained loamy soil.
  • Sunlight: Requires full to partial sunlight.
  • Watering: Moderate watering, avoiding waterlogging.

Uses

  • Ornamental: Adds beauty to gardens and courtyards.
  • Religious: Widely used for temple offerings and garlands.
  • Dye Production: Orange corolla is used to produce a natural dye.

Interesting Facts

  • The name *arbor-tristis* means "sad tree," referring to flowers that fall by morning.
  • It is not a true jasmine and does not belong to the genus *Jasminum*.
  • The Coral Jasmine is the state flower of West Bengal, India.
